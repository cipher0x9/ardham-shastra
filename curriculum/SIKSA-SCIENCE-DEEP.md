## 1. Ebbinghaus, Forgetting Curves, Spacing

### 1.1 Core finding (SOURCE-FAITHFUL to experimental tradition)

Hermann Ebbinghaus (1885, *Über das Gedächtnis*) measured retention of nonsense syllables and established:

1. **Forgetting is rapid early, then slows** — a negatively accelerated curve.
2. **Relearning is faster than initial learning** — savings method.
3. **Distributed practice beats massed practice** for long retention.

**BRIDGE to campus:** We treat the forgetting curve as a *design constraint*, not a moral failure. Spacing is how you outrun decay.

### 1.2 Spacing effect — operational rules

| Condition | Massed | Spaced (expanding) | Spaced (fixed) |
|-----------|--------|--------------------|----------------|
| Short test (minutes) | Often wins | Mixed | Mixed |
| Long retention (days+) | Weak | Strong | Strong |
| Transfer | Weak | Stronger | Stronger |
| Illusion of mastery | High | Lower | Lower |

**Recommended expanding schedule (INVENTION — campus default):**

```
T0 learn → T+10min → T+1d → T+3d → T+7d → T+14d → T+30d → T+90d
```

Map to SM-2 / Anki: see MEMORY-SCIENCE-SYSTEMS.md.

### 1.3 Classical parallel (BRIDGE)

Vedic oral culture used **daily, fortnightly, and seasonal re-recitation** of corpus material. Vikṛti pāṭha variants (jaṭā, ghana) force re-encoding under high load — a cultural technology of spacing + retrieval + error-checking.

## 2. Retrieval Practice (Testing Effect)

### 2.1 Evidence core (SOURCE-FAITHFUL to modern lit)

Roediger & Karpicke and successors: **active retrieval strengthens memory more than re-study**, even when retrieval is incomplete — if feedback follows.

Mechanisms (simplified):

- Retrieval is a **memory modifier**, not just a readout.
- Successful retrieval strengthens cue–target links.
- Failed retrieval + corrective feedback still teaches diagnostic discrimination.

### 2.2 Practice designs

| Mode | How | When to use | Risk |
|------|-----|-------------|------|
| Free recall | Blank page, dump all | End of session | Incomplete coverage |
| Cued recall | Prompt → answer | Cards, sūtras | Over-cued |
| Recognition | MCQ / true-false | Fast screening | False fluency |
| Generation | Produce example | Transfer | High load |
| Teach-back | Explain to peer/AI | Mastery check | Social anxiety |

**Campus rule (INVENTION):** Closed-book first; open notes only after first retrieval attempt fails.

### 2.3 Classical bridge

**Svādhyāya** (self-study/recitation) and **guru examination** are retrieval machines. The student who only *hears* is not the student who can *produce* the sūtra under pressure.

## 3. Interleaving

### 3.1 Blocked vs interleaved practice

| Schedule | Example | Immediate performance | Retention/transfer |
|----------|---------|----------------------|--------------------|
| Blocked | AAA BBB CCC | Feels easy, high | Weaker discrimination |
| Interleaved | ABC ACB BAC | Feels harder | Stronger discrimination |

Rohrer, Taylor, and math-education studies: interleaving improves **category discrimination** (which procedure when).

### 3.2 How to interleave without chaos

1. **Within-domain interleave first** — quadratic vs factoring vs systems, not random subjects.
2. **Keep a skeleton of spaced pure blocks** for fragile new skills.
3. **Use “mix after acquisition”** — first 20% blocked for orientation, then mix.

**BRIDGE:** Multi-śāstra days (grammar + logic + math) are interleaved *domains*; within a math hour, interleave *problem types*.

## 4. Desirable Difficulties (Bjork)

### 4.1 Definition (SOURCE-FAITHFUL framing)

A **desirable difficulty** is a condition that:

- slows or disrupts short-term performance,
- improves long-term retention and transfer,
- is *desirable* only if the learner can still succeed with effort (not impossible).

Examples:

| Difficulty | Why it helps | When it fails |
|------------|--------------|---------------|
| Spacing | Forces re-encoding | Gaps too long → total loss |
| Interleaving | Discrimination learning | Novice overload |
| Varied contexts | Flexible cues | No stable core yet |
| Generation | Deeper processing | No feedback → errors fossilize |
| Reduced feedback | Independent monitoring | Permanent wrong models |

### 4.2 Tapas mapping (BRIDGE)

Classical **tapas** is not self-harm; it is sustained effort that transforms capacity. Desirable difficulty is the cognitive-science cousin: effort that purchases durable skill.

## 5. Sleep Consolidation

### 5.1 Mechanisms (BRIDGE to mainstream neuroscience)

- **Slow-wave sleep (SWS):** systems consolidation, hippocampal → neocortical transfer for declarative content.
- **REM:** procedural refinement, emotional tagging, creative recombination.
- **Sleep deprivation:** impairs encoding next day and consolidation of prior day.

### 5.2 Operational protocol (INVENTION — campus)

```
Evening (T-60min sleep): light retrieval of day's hardest 5 items (no new bulk)
Sleep: 7–9h, consistent schedule
Morning (brahmamuhūrta window optional): 10–20min free recall + hard problem
Nap (if available): 20–90min after heavy learning block
```

**Do not** all-night cram before exams; protect sleep as part of the study plan (see EXAM-PERFORMANCE-ENGINE.md).

## 6. Expertise Research (Ericsson) — Deliberate Practice

### 6.1 Deliberate practice criteria (SOURCE-FAITHFUL to Ericsson framework)

1. **Specific goal** improving a well-defined aspect of performance.
2. **Focused attention** (not autopilot reps).
3. **Immediate informative feedback**.
4. **Repetition with refinement** outside comfort zone.
5. **Mental representations** that improve (better internal models).

Not the same as: naive practice, play, or experience alone (10,000 hours of poor practice ≠ expertise).

### 6.2 Guru role

| Function | Classical | Modern |
|----------|-----------|--------|
| Task selection | Guru assigns next sūtra/skill | Coach designs drills |
| Feedback | Immediate correction | Video, tests, AI critique |
| Motivation | Discipline + relationship | SDT-supportive coaching |
| Representation | Oral models, demonstration | Worked examples, rubrics |

### 6.3 Mental representations checklist

- Can you **predict** errors before they happen?
- Can you **chunk** the domain into higher-order patterns?
- Can you **self-correct** without external feedback most of the time?
- Can you **teach** the structure, not only the steps?

## 7. Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller et al.)

### 7.1 Three loads

| Load | Definition | Design response |
|------|------------|-----------------|
| Intrinsic | Element interactivity of the material | Sequence, pre-train, segment |
| Extraneous | Poor instructional design | Clean UI, no fluff, dual-channel carefully |
| Germane | Effort building schemas | Free capacity for this by cutting extraneous |

### 7.2 Rules of thumb

1. **Novices:** worked examples > pure problem solving (see §10).
2. **Split-attention:** integrate text with diagram; don't force visual search.
3. **Redundancy:** don't narrate identical text on screen (for multimedia).
4. **Modality:** spoken words + diagram can beat written words + diagram when visual channel is busy.
5. **Expertise reversal:** what helps novices can hurt experts — fade scaffolds.

**BRIDGE to sūtra pedagogy:** Ultra-compressed sūtras have high intrinsic load; the **vṛtti/bhāṣya/udāharaṇa** ladder is classical load management.

## 8. Metacognition

### 8.1 Components

| Phase | Question | Tool |
|-------|----------|------|
| Planning | What do I need? What's hard? | Pre-test, goal card |
| Monitoring | Am I understanding? | Self-explain, confidence ratings |
| Evaluation | Did I meet the goal? | Post-test, error log |
| Regulation | What do I change next? | Weekly review |

### 8.2 Calibration

People are often **overconfident** after re-reading (fluency illusion) and **underconfident** after hard retrieval that actually builds memory.

**Fix:** confidence rating *before* looking up answer; track calibration weekly.

```
Item | My answer | Confidence 1-5 | Correct? | Calibration note
-----|-----------|----------------|----------|------------------
```

### 8.3 Svādhyāya as metacognition (BRIDGE)

Self-study in classical sense includes honesty about what is not yet firm — the opposite of performative “I already know this.”

## 9. Self-Explanation

### 9.1 Chi’s self-explanation effect

Learners who explain *why* a step works outperform those who only read solutions — especially for transfer.

### 9.2 Protocol

After each worked step or sūtra application, answer:

1. **What** was done?
2. **Why** this move, not alternatives?
3. **When** would this fail?
4. **What principle** generalizes?

**Campus Feynman check (INVENTION):** Explain to a 12-year-old / to a peer / to an AI that is instructed to attack gaps.

## 10. Worked Examples & Fading

### 10.1 Example–problem pairs

For novices: study a full solution, then solve an isomorphic problem. Better than pure problem-solving under high load.

### 10.2 Fading sequence (INVENTION template)

```
Stage A: Fully worked example + self-explain each step
Stage B: Partial example (last steps blank)
Stage C: Problem with hints available
Stage D: Independent problem
Stage E: Varied / interleaved problems
Stage F: Generate a new problem for someone else
```

### 10.3 Classical ladder (BRIDGE)

Sūtra → vṛtti → example → counterexample → student production. Same fading logic across millennia.

## 11. Feedback Timing

| Task type | Prefer | Rationale |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Motor / procedure acquisition | Immediate | Prevent error fossilization |
| Conceptual discrimination | Slightly delayed OK | Forces processing |
| High-stakes formative | Immediate + explanation | Correct mental model |
| Summative exam simulation | Delayed until end | Exam realism |
| Creative work | Process feedback mid, product at milestones | Preserve agency |

**Three feedback levels (Hattie/Timperley framing, BRIDGE):**

1. **Task** — correct/incorrect, what to fix.
2. **Process** — which strategy to use.
3. **Self-regulation** — how to monitor yourself next time.

Avoid empty praise feedback (“great job”) without task information.

## 12. Motivation Science

### 12.1 Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan) — BRIDGE

| Need | Meaning | Study design |
|------|---------|--------------|
| Autonomy | Choice, ownership | Choose project within curriculum rails |
| Competence | Effective progress | Visible proof artifacts, small wins |
| Relatedness | Belonging | Study partner, mentor, public mission |

### 12.2 Goal systems

- **Mastery goals** > pure performance goals for long learning.
- **Implementation intentions:** “If situation X, then I do Y” beats vague goals.
- **Identity-based habits:** “I am a person who does morning retrieval” beats “I should study more.”

### 12.3 Purushārtha mapping (BRIDGE)

| Classical aim | Learning motivation use |
|---------------|-------------------------|
| Dharma | Duty to craft / students / truth |
| Artha | Career skill, portfolio proof |
| Kāma | Joy of insight, aesthetic of elegance |
| Mokṣa | Freedom from confusion, clarity |

Campus ethics: **plant, do not sell** — motivation without predatory monetization of confusion.

### 12.4 Grit & interest

Long arcs need **sustained interest** + **deliberate practice**. Passion without method plateaus; method without meaning dies.

## 13. Learning Styles Debunked

### 13.1 The meshing hypothesis

Claim: teaching in a student’s preferred “style” (V/A/K etc.) improves learning.

**Evidence status:** Large reviews (Pashler et al., and later syntheses) find **no adequate support** for meshing. People have *preferences*, not fixed channels that determine optimal instruction.

### 13.2 What to do instead

1. Match **modality to content** (diagrams for spatial relations; audio for phonology).
2. Use **dual coding** thoughtfully (Paivio) — words + relevant visuals.
3. Respect **individual differences** in prior knowledge, working memory, interest — not mystical styles.
4. Give **choice** for autonomy, not because style quizzes are scientific.

**Label:** SOURCE-FAITHFUL to the skeptical consensus on meshing; BRIDGE in campus policy: preferences welcome, science first.

## 14. Integrated Daily Protocol (INVENTION)

### 14.1 90-minute deep block

```
0–5 min   Goal + pre-test (retrieval of yesterday)
5–40 min  New material: worked examples + self-explain
40–55 min  Generation / problem solving (desirable difficulty)
55–65 min  Interleaved mini-set from prior topics
65–75 min  Free recall dump + error log
75–85 min  Spaced card review (Anki/SM-2)
85–90 min  Metacognitive note: what to change tomorrow
```

## 18. Worked Case — Learning a Technical Domain in 30 Days

**Domain example:** SIP call setup (or any protocol stack).

| Week | Science lever | Action |
|------|---------------|--------|
| 1 | Worked examples + low load | Trace INVITE→200 OK fully worked; self-explain |
| 2 | Retrieval + generation | Draw call flow from memory; fail; correct |
| 3 | Interleave | Mix REGISTER, INVITE, REFER, failure cases |
| 4 | Deliberate practice + proof | Lab captures, error diagnosis log, teach-back |

Measure: closed-book flow accuracy, time-to-diagnose, peer teach score.
