Suggested order

Study path

Walk the campus in the order it was built. At each step, do the practice gate before you call the module done. One domain, practised, beats forty modules skimmed.

Short path, from the opening notes

  1. Read the vow in Module 01 and say what Ardham means.
  2. Learn the loop in Module 02: expose, engage, retrieve, apply, prove, teach. Close the source and produce it yourself.
  3. Pick one thing. Write the seven steps in Module 08 as this week’s actions. Do the map today.
  4. Put Module 16 on a calendar. START-HERE prints the same seven phases (days 1–7 through days 78–90). In the campus file that arc is Module 16, not Module 10. Module 10 is Cosmos and Beyond.
  5. Prove as you go. RTMA: run, trace, metric, artifact. LICC: leg, id, counter, capture.
  6. Re-read a module after 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days, and 90 days.

The full pass below follows campus order, 01 through 40. Cross-links make a lattice, not only a line.

Core method

Modules 01–08 are how the campus opens. Everything later is this method pointed at a field.

Step 01 · Core method

The Path — Why This Exists

Ardham Shastra (అర్థం శాస్త్రం — the science of meaning) is the master-method campus. The promise: there is a method to master anything.

Practise. In one sentence, explain why this campus exists and what the word Ardham means. Then teach it to someone in 60 seconds.

Step 02 · Core method

Śikṣā — The Science of Learning

Śikṣā (शिक्षा) is the first of the six Vedāṅgas. It began as precise pronunciation so knowledge could cross generations without corruption. Learning is retrieval, spacing, and use.

Practise. Set a review schedule for one topic: 1h → 24h → 7d → 30d → 90d. After the 24h review, write the Śikṣā loop from memory.

Step 03 · Core method

Pāṇini — The Grammar of Everything

The Aṣṭādhyāyī is a generative grammar: a small set of rules that produce infinite valid forms. Every domain has a grammar. Find it and you stop memorizing instances.

Practise. Map one domain into utsarga, apavāda, and siddha: the general rule, two exceptions, and one verified output. Teach the grammar-to-computation connection in 60 seconds.

Step 04 · Core method

Nyāya — The Logic Engine

Nyāya is the science of reasoning. The Nyāya-sūtra of Gautama opens with 16 categories. Correct reasoning was the path to correct knowledge, and correct knowledge was liberation from error.

Practise. Take one belief. Run it through all five hetvābhāsa checks. Write the five-member syllogism for one real claim and mark the weakest step.

Step 05 · Core method

Vaiśeṣika and Sāṃkhya — Maps of Reality

Two map-making tools for any domain: categories of what exists, and layers from observer to physical matter.

Practise. Map your domain with Vaiśeṣika’s six categories and Sāṃkhya’s layer stack. Name the layer that usually breaks when you troubleshoot.

Step 06 · Core method

The Jñāna Mathematics

India’s mathematical tradition is intuition first, notation second. Deep thinking needs clean reasoning, not modern symbols as a prerequisite.

Practise. Compute the mātrā-meru up to n = 7 by hand. Derive π ≈ 3.1416 with an inscribed polygon, or explain why the Kerala series converges to π. Teach the mātrā-meru story in 60 seconds.

Step 07 · Core method

The Memory Arsenal

The śikṣā tradition transmitted the Vedas — millions of syllables — by redundant encoding. The same moves still work: compress, lattice, story, palace, and spaced review.

Practise. Create five memory cards for today. Review one jaṭā-style, forward and backward. Compute the SM-2 interval for a card you rated 5.

Step 17 · Domain grammar

Language Mastery

Language is the Pāṇini method in the open: a finite grammar generates infinite sentences. The campus runs nine languages as separate sound-spaces on one shared skeleton.

Practise. Build a one-page grammar map for your target language. Create 20 bidirectional word cards. Record a 30-second shadow. Teach the SOV versus SVO contrast.

Step 18 · Domain grammar

Mathematics Deep

Mathematics is a grammar: a few axioms generate infinite theorems. Knowing a result has levels, and derivation is the minimum for anything core.

Practise. Derive the quadratic formula from scratch, without peeking. Compute π to three decimals with the Kerala series. Prove 1 + 2 + … + n by induction. Teach the levels of understanding.
